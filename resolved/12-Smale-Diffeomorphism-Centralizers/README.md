# Smale Problem #12: Centralizers of Diffeomorphisms (Resolved in $C^1$)

## Problem Statement
Is the centralizer of a generic diffeomorphism of a compact smooth manifold trivial (i.e., consisting only of the powers of the diffeomorphism)?
That is, for a generic $f \in \operatorname{Diff}^r(M)$, is $Z(f) \coloneqq \{ g \in \operatorname{Diff}^r(M) \mid g \circ f = f \circ g \} = \{ f^k \mid k \in \mathbb{Z} \}$?

## Resolution History
- **Formulated by**: Steve Smale (1967, 1998, 2000).
- **Proved in $C^1$ topology by**: **Christian Bonatti, Sylvain Crovisier, and Amie Wilkinson** (2009):
  * *The $C^1$-generic diffeomorphism has trivial centralizer*, Publications Mathématiques de l'IHÉS, Vol. 109, pp. 185–244 (2009).
- **Key Breakthrough**: In the $C^1$ topology, for every compact manifold $M$, there is a residual (dense $G_\delta$) set of diffeomorphisms whose centralizer is discrete and minimal: $Z(f) = \langle f \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}$ (or $\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}_2$ for non-orientable cases).
- **Status for $r \ge 2$**: Open in higher regularity $C^r$ ($r \ge 2$), though proved for conservative and Axiom A systems.
